# Access Curriculum & Technology

A dependency-free static website for Access's computing course-development services, offered by Alex Shaw and Baha Marupov. No build step, external fonts, analytics, or services are required.

## Core message and structure

Alex Shaw and Baha Marupov are each software professionals and college educators who help colleges **modernize existing computing courses or create new ones**, review existing computing programs, and keep curricula current through annual partnerships. The first screen states that offer and gives both names equal prominence. Keep future changes focused on this message.

The homepage has four destinations: **Services**, **Experience**, **Course example**, and **Contact Alex**. Services opens with the origin story: teaching at colleges showed Alex and Baha how course updates compete with teaching and student support for faculty time as technology changes. Access helps with that work through three services: review and update existing courses/programs, create a new computing course, and keep courses/programs current through an Annual Curriculum Partnership. Compensation differences appear only as supporting context. Experience introduces both people; named organizations describe Alex's individual work history. The featured AI course example demonstrates teaching materials and inspectable software on its own page. Contact welcomes both defined projects and annual partnerships and provides Alex's email and a one-page overview.

Use professional, everyday language and company voice ("we") for services. Keep the story within the Services introduction, without adding another navigation item or large section. Both people have confirmed software and college teaching experience; do not invent additional credentials for either person.

Keep the presentation minimal: one hero paragraph, a compact equal introduction of both people, three service columns with visible deliverables, one shared working-terms block, and one course-example feature. Use the existing green/ivory/lime palette and serif accents with restrained rules. Avoid repeating professional labels, service slogans, decorative card icons, and links to the same destination within a section. Keep substantive scope, biography facts, sample qualifications, and faculty approval visible; do not hide them in accordions or rely on the PDF alone.

## Service boundaries

The first service is a defined review/update project, covering course content, tools, learning goals, prerequisites, course sequence, priorities, and agreed material revisions. New development remains focused on courses, not new certificates or degree programs. The Annual Curriculum Partnership includes an annual review, technology/workplace changes, agreed updates and faculty consultation during the year, a record of changes, remaining priorities, and a refreshed plan. Covered courses/program, included revisions, consultation, schedule, and fee are agreed in advance; additional work is scoped separately. Internal catalog prices and hours are planning examples, not public rates or unlimited-revision promises.

Show the three service panels in equal columns above 1100px and stack them at 1100px and below. Keep the four navigation destinations unchanged. Both the annual service and Contact section link to Alex with the subject "Annual Curriculum Partnership".

## Preview and hosting

Run `python tools/preview.py` and visit `http://127.0.0.1:8765`. The preview binds only to loopback and serves an explicit list of public site assets; it does not expose directory listings, Git data, or tooling. Stop it with Ctrl+C when finished. Use this HTTP preview for the AI interaction, which loads the shared JSON cases. Essential teaching content remains readable without JavaScript.

The live site is [GitHub Pages](https://alexandergshaw.github.io/curriculum-and-technology-services-website/). Completed changes are committed and pushed to `main`; verify the published content and downloads after pushing. To use another static host, upload `index.html`, `ai-course-example.html`, `curriculum-sample.html`, `styles.css`, `ai-example.css`, `sample.css`, `script.js`, `ai-example.js`, `favicon.svg`, `teaching-sample/data/cases.json`, `downloads/`, and `output/pdf/` together, keeping relative paths intact. `tools/` and `output/previews/` are local development resources and are not needed on the host. The Python teaching sample is a downloadable local exercise, not a website backend.
