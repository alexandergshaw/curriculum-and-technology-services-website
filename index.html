Teaching experience

Connecting a web interface to a working application

Web development / INFO 2589

Connecting frontend and backend takes more than syntax. Alex's instruction progresses from guided TypeScript/React and Express tasks to an Events App assignment, compilation/testing, GitHub submissions, and screenshots of running work.

Design focus: Running code and documented implementation make application development assessable.

Explore the web development activity

Illustrative curriculum sample

Retrieve an event list from an Express endpoint and display it in React. Include loading, empty, and error states.

Submit: Code and a short demonstration tracing a request from interface to endpoint and back.

Assess: Correct data flow, usable states, and a clear explanation of the implementation.

Curriculum design example

Turning systems concepts into troubleshooting practice

Systems administration / INFO 2451

Systems knowledge needs a practical test. INFO 2451 materials cover Windows/Linux administration, permissions, Active Directory, network services, virtualization, security, and recovery. Related planning pairs demonstrations and guided labs with quizzes and troubleshooting evidence.

Design focus: Connect configuration decisions to observable checks.

Explore the troubleshooting activity

Illustrative curriculum sample

Diagnose an access-permission issue in an isolated training environment. Make a justified change, then verify authorized access without broadening unrelated permissions.

Submit: Diagnosis, configuration evidence, and repeatable validation steps.

Assess: Reasoning, appropriate permissions, and verification of the fix.

Course proposal

Applying AI to a business workflow

Applied AI / Phased pathway

Begin with task fit, not a tool. Alex's proposed course combines AI fluency, prompting, output evaluation, process analysis, no-code automation, and responsible use: accuracy, privacy, and bias.

It proposes a project with a local Midwest business or nonprofit team and a portfolio artifact; a later technical course would cover AI application development and deployment.

Design focus: Sequence practical judgment before technical depth.

Explore the AI workflow activity

Illustrative curriculum sample

Use fictional or synthetic documents to map a workflow and prototype an AI-assisted step. Evaluate outputs for accuracy and bias; identify human-review points.

Submit: Workflow map, prototype, and evaluation notes.

Assess: Task fit, output checking, and responsible information handling.

03 / Approach

A manageable way to begin.

A focused review, single-course project, or phased engagement can be a starting point.

  1. 01

    Understand the need

    Discuss priorities, students, faculty capacity, existing materials, and constraints.

  2. 02

    Agree on the work

    Define scope, deliverables, responsibilities, review points, timing, and budget.

  3. 03

    Build, review, and hand over

    Develop with faculty input. Provide agreed materials and implementation guidance, with ongoing support if scoped.

Useful work starts with a realistic scope.

We discuss your priorities and available resources, then propose clear deliverables and a manageable path forward.

A smaller budget may call for a smaller or phased scope.

04 / About

Experience in the classroom and in software delivery

Access Curriculum & Technology is a founder-led consulting practice based in Omaha, Nebraska, working with colleges across the Midwest.

Alex Shaw brings enterprise software-engineering leadership, including lead engineering work at Mutual of Omaha, alongside teaching and curriculum-development experience at Metropolitan College and Midland University in Fremont, Nebraska.

Those Midwest roots inform our purpose: help regional colleges address practical gaps, support faculty expertise, and create learning experiences students can demonstrate to local and regional employers. We work collaboratively, with usable materials and a clear handoff.

A few practical details

Before we talk.

Can we start with one course?

Yes. A refresh, new build, or focused review can be scoped independently.

How much faculty involvement is needed?

We agree on subject-matter input, review points, and handoff at the start.

Can you work with our LMS?

We confirm platform, format, access, and implementation scope before committing.

Can we edit and reuse the materials?

We plan for editable deliverables and handoff. Ownership, licensing, and reuse terms are documented in the agreement, including any third-party resources.

Do you work with colleges outside Nebraska?

Yes. We're based in Omaha and partner with colleges across Iowa, Kansas, Missouri, South Dakota, and the wider Midwest—remotely, with on-campus sessions available for regional institutions.
