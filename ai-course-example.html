01 / What students learn

Build a system.
Understand its limits.

A focused applied elective

For students who can already write and debug a small program, read structured data, and use basic Git. A readiness exercise checks those foundations. We supply an application scaffold so students can focus on retrieval, responses, and evaluation.

A clear progression

AI foundations and a guided classification exercise lead into search, model integration, evaluation, and maintenance. The full course outline is in the download. Credit structure, prerequisites, and final outcomes are agreed with faculty.

Build
Retrieve relevant passages and preserve their sources; in the full course, integrate a model-backed answer path.
Evaluate
Compare approaches using documented cases, check whether sources support claims, and explain the results.
Revise and explain
Diagnose a failure, make and test a correction, and demonstrate individual understanding through an observed change.

02 / What students build

An IT support assistant.
With evidence behind it.

A fictional college supplies a small collection of support articles. Students first build ordinary search, then compare answers against those sources. The full course adds live model integration. The public unit uses authored responses so every student can study the same failures without an API account.

Working code you can inspect

The download contains a Python search baseline, a clearly named replay component, starter tasks, a reference solution, and automated tests. It runs offline using the standard library. Students work on source handling, response validation, evaluation, and revision.

Judgment beyond a passing test

Code can check response structure and source versions. A person still needs to judge whether a passage supports an answer. That distinction is part of the assignment and rubric.

03 / How learning is assessed

The reasoning matters
as much as the result.

Students submit their implementation, evaluation cases, a justified revision, and a change record. Each student explains a decision and makes an observed modification, with an accessible equivalent available. The sample includes an annotated submission with partial-credit reasoning.

Implementation and source handling
25%
Evaluation design and interpretation
30%
Failure handling and justified revisions
15%
Reproducibility and handoff
10%
Individual explanation and modification
20%

Illustrative weights; faculty approve thresholds and essential individual competencies. Students may use AI assistance under a stated policy, explain what they accepted, and show how they verified it. Grades reflect work students control, not a model's comparative answer score.

Choosing the simpler solution can be the right answer.

In the full course, students must implement and evaluate both search and a model-backed approach. They may then recommend either. Quality, appropriate refusal, source support, and consistency are considered together; cost and response time are reported separately. Any measured results must retain their inputs, configuration, outputs, and limitations.

04 / How faculty teach and update it

Ready to inspect.
Designed to adapt.

Included in this sample

One complete offline unit: student instructions, working starter and reference code, synthetic data, tests, rubric, annotated assessment, instructor preparation and troubleshooting notes, and a revision record. The proposed semester outline is included separately.

Agreed before course delivery

The full course needs institution-approved model access, usage limits, and an assigned setup contact. Replay supports preparation and outages; it does not prove live integration. Students receive equivalent access or another opportunity to demonstrate that outcome. The notes include an access checklist and cost-planning worksheet.

Keeping the materials current

A source changes. The lesson changes with it.

The sample includes two versions of a support article. An old answer can still look convincing after its instructions change. A regression test exposes the stale citation; the revision selects the current source and records what needs human review.

  1. 01Review the change

    Compare the original article with its replacement.

  2. 02Revise and test

    Update the implementation, assignment, and expected evidence.

  3. 03Review with faculty

    Record changes, approval decisions, and remaining priorities.

The included change record is illustrative; no real faculty approval is claimed. It models our annual partnership cycle: review together, agree priorities, update materials, and review the changes with faculty.
